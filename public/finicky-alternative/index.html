Finicky without the config file

Updated 17 November 2026

Finicky by John Sterling is a great open source browser router. You describe every rule in a JavaScript or TypeScript file, so it can do almost anything. Detour takes the opposite route: it learns where your links belong from your own browsing history and you set it up with clicks. Both are free and MIT licensed.

Where Finicky is better

If you like writing rules, Finicky is hard to beat. A rule can be any function, so you can rewrite links before they open, match on the time of day or a held key, and keep the whole setup in your dotfiles. Its Chrome and Firefox extensions also catch links you click inside a browser, which Detour can't do yet.

Where Detour is better

Finicky only knows what you tell it. Detour also looks at where you opened each site before, per profile, per app you clicked in and per workspace (github.com/your-company versus github.com/you). Most links need no rule at all, and when Detour really isn't sure it shows a picker instead of guessing. Settings show you why each link went where it did.

Switching from Finicky

Install Detour, run the two-minute setup and make it your default browser. Setup finds your browsers and profiles and suggests rules from your history, so you don't have to port your config. Your ~/.finicky.js stays where it is, and to go back you pick Finicky as your default browser again.
